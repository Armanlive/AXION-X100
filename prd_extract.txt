78.1 Screen perception
After explicit user authorization, AXION may capture the current screen or a selected application/window and use visual understanding to reason about what the user is seeing, correlating it with authorized project/code context. Example: "AXION, look at my screen and tell me why this error is happening." Screen content is untrusted visual input, including any instructions displayed within it. Seeing something never grants permission to interact with it. Capture scope must be explicit and limited to the authorized screen/window and session.
78.2 Camera perception
Camera access requires explicit permission. Potential uses include visual troubleshooting, understanding physical objects/devices, reading information intentionally presented by the user, and multimodal conversation. Camera activation must always have a persistent visible indicator. No hidden camera access or surveillance. Ending or revoking the session must stop capture.
78.3 Computer Control subsystem
A dedicated subsystem may eventually support observing a screen, locating a UI target, clicking, double-clicking, right-clicking, scrolling, typing, keyboard shortcuts, drag/drop, and switching supported applications/windows. Never blindly execute coordinates emitted by an LLM. Required action path:
Perception  Action Plan  Policy/Risk Check  Grounded Target  Action  Observe Result  Verify
Ground the target in current application/window evidence before acting. If the target is ambiguous or the screen has changed, observe again rather than guessing.

78.4 Closed-loop computer use

OBSERVE  UNDERSTAND  PLAN  AUTHORIZE  ACT  OBSERVE AGAIN  VERIFY

A click or keystroke being issued is not proof of success. Post-action screen/application state is evidence. Failed or unverified actions must be reported as such, with immediate cancellation available.
78.5 Capability-scoped permissions and risk tiers
"Full laptop access" must not mean unrestricted permanent authority. Permissions must be capability-, application/device-, scope-, and session-bound, revocable, and enforced outside model output. Lower-risk actions may proceed under an explicit user policy; sensitive or destructive actions require the corresponding explicit authorization.
